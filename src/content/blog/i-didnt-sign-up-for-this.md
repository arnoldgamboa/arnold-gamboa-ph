I'm a bivocational pastor. It's not hard to explain. I pastor a local church, and I also have a full-time job. The pastoring is volunteer work, and nobody pays me for it.

It's a strange kind of volunteering, though. Most volunteers sign up. They see a need, raise a hand, and give a Saturday. I didn't really sign up for this. Most bivocational pastors I know would say the same thing: we do it because we believe God called us to it. That's the only reason that holds up. It isn't the pay, and some weeks it isn't the thanks either.

## Preaching is the easy part

People sometimes assume the sermon is the hardest part of pastoring. For me it's the easiest. That doesn't make it light work. I care so much about what I get to preach that I spend hours preparing for about forty minutes on Sunday, and I think it matters a great deal. But it's work I understand. The text is there, the time is mine, and I know what to do with it.

What drains me emotionally, physically, and even spiritually is people. I say that carefully, because the people are the point.

It's keeping people engaged. It's dealing with pride and anger, and seeing how that affects the people around them. It's handling everyone's different agendas for what the church should be. It's money. I'm thankful for the generosity of the people in our church, but often it still isn't enough. And it's spiritual growth. Some people grow. Some are stubborn and refuse to, and you can't make them.

None of that fits in a sermon outline, and you can't prepare for it on a Saturday night.

## Why I'm still here

There have been plenty of times I thought about quitting. I won't pretend otherwise. Quitting is always an option, and nobody is keeping me there.

But I keep coming back to the same thing. I didn't sign up for this, so I don't get to sign off just because it's hard. I was called. That doesn't make God love me more, and walking away wouldn't make Him love me less. But it does settle who gets to say I'm done.

So here's where I've landed.

Until God tells me to stop, I'll keep going.

Until the people God has given me to pastor ask me to stop and let me sit down, I'll keep doing this.

Until I'm no longer able, I will.

That isn't a heroic statement. Most weeks it looks ordinary: a sermon finished late, a hard conversation, a budget that doesn't quite close, a message to someone who stopped coming. Faithfulness usually looks like that.
