I'm a bivocational pastor. It's not hard to explain. I pastor a local church, and I also have a full-time job. The pastoring is volunteer work, and nobody pays me for it.

It's a strange kind of volunteering, though. Most volunteers sign up. They see a need, raise a hand, and give a Saturday. I didn't really sign up for this. Most bivocational pastors I know would say the same thing: we do it because we believe God called us to it. That's the reason that holds everything else up.

## Preaching is the easy part

People sometimes assume the sermon is the hardest part of pastoring. For me it's the easiest. That doesn't make it light work. I care so much about what I get to preach that I spend hours preparing for about forty minutes on Sunday. But it's work I understand. The text is there, the time is mine, and I know what to do with it.

Shepherding people is different. People aren't a project you can prepare for on a Saturday night. Each person comes with a story, hurts, hopes, and a pace of growth that's theirs and not mine. Caring for them well means listening, being patient, sitting in hard conversations, and trusting God with the parts I can't fix. It means carrying the church's needs, including the practical ones like money, where I'm grateful for our people's generosity even when it isn't quite enough. And it means remembering I'm one of the sheep too, still being shaped by the same grace I preach.

That's the part that stretches me the most. It's also the part I'd least want to give up.

I won't pretend it's always easy. There have been times I thought about quitting, and quitting is always an option. But the hard parts aren't the main story.

## Why I'm still here

The main reason I stay is simple. I'm thankful for the calling God has given me. I love our people, the ones God has entrusted to me. I love seeing them grow. I love seeing them love God more. Those moments don't show up on a schedule, but when they come, they make every late sermon and hard conversation worth it.

Being called doesn't make God love me more, and stepping away wouldn't make Him love me less. It's a gift, not a way to earn anything. That's what makes it a joy and not just a duty.

So here's where I've landed.

As long as God wants me to take care of these people, I will.

As long as He wants me to take care of this church, I'll keep showing up.

And until He says otherwise, I'm thankful I get to.
