Rest has a way of turning into a prize.

Finish the list. Close the tickets. Get through the week. Then you can sit down. Then you can sleep without guilt. Then you can take a walk that is not secretly another way to think about work.

I know that bargain. I have kept it more often than I want to admit.

The problem is that the list never quite ends. There is always one more thing that would make rest feel earned. A cleaner inbox. A shipped feature. A Sunday that went smoothly. A week that finally looked like competence.

If rest has to be deserved, it will keep moving.

## Work can ask. It cannot own the whole week.

Work is allowed to ask for attention. A team needs it. A church needs it. People who depend on you need it. That is not the enemy.

The enemy is the quiet rule that says I am only allowed to stop when I have nothing left unfinished.

I will never have nothing left unfinished. Neither will you.

Some of the work in front of us is good. Some of it is ordinary. Some of it will still be there on Monday. None of that is a verdict on whether we may rest tonight.

A job can be unfinished and still be faithful. A sermon can be unfinished and still be honest. A house can be unfinished and still be a place to sleep.

## Rest is part of the work of being human

I am not arguing for laziness. I still care about showing up. I still want to do the next right thing well.

But a life that only rests after it has won is a life that will stay tired on purpose.

For Christians, rest is not a productivity hack. It is a way of telling the truth: I am not the one who keeps the world running. The standing I have with God is not improved by a late night and not reduced by a nap.

That does not make the work small. It puts the work back in its place.

You can close the laptop with items still open. You can leave a good idea on the list. You can go home before you have become impressive.

That is not falling behind.

It is refusing to treat rest like a prize you have to win first.
