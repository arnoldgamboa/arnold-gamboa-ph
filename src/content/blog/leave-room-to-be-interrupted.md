Monday mornings tempt me to plan the whole week down to the half hour. Block the calendar. Line up the meetings. Decide what gets done by Friday and protect it from everything else.

There is nothing wrong with a plan. I need one. My week already has a lot living in it: the job, studying for Sunday, my wife, my kids.

But I have noticed something about the two kinds of work I do. As a manager and as a pastor, a lot of the real work does not show up on the calendar. It shows up as an interruption.

## The work that arrives unscheduled

A teammate asks, "Do you have a minute?" and it is not about the ticket. A message comes in late from someone in church who does not want to talk about it on Sunday. A short call turns into a long one because the person on the other end finally said the real thing.

None of that was on my list. Most of it is the job.

I have written before that preaching is the easy part of pastoring and caring for people is the harder part. Part of what makes it hard is that people do not need you on schedule. They need you when they need you. Listening takes time. Patience takes time. You cannot squeeze a hard conversation into the eight minutes left before the next meeting.

So if every hour of my week is already spoken for, I have quietly decided that nobody else gets to need me.

A day can be efficient and still miss the people in it.

## Slack is not wasted time

I think about how often Jesus was interrupted. He was on His way to one need when another one reached out and touched Him. The Gospels do not treat those moments as detours. Often they are the point of the story.

I am not Jesus, and I cannot be available to everyone all the time. I have also written about wanting more room for myself, for quiet, reflection, and old friends. That has not changed. Leaving room to be interrupted is not the same as having no boundaries. It is deciding ahead of time that some of my margin belongs to people I cannot schedule yet.

So this week I am trying something small. I am leaving a few gaps in the calendar on purpose. Not for a project. Not for catching up. Just space, so when someone knocks, I am not already late for something else.

Some weeks that space will sit empty, and I will use it to think or read. That is fine too.

I do not want my faithfulness measured by how full my calendar is. My standing with God does not rise when I finish the list, and it does not fall when someone's need takes the afternoon. That was settled in Christ before Monday started.

Which means I am free to be interrupted.
