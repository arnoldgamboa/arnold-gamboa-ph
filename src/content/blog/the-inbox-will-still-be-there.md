Monday mornings usually start the same way. Open the laptop. Scan the inbox. Check what spilled over from last week. Feel the quiet pressure to get ahead of the day before the day gets ahead of me.

Today looks a little different.

Yesterday my daughter turned eighteen. We celebrated. My son was there. My wife was there. It was the kind of day that doesn't care about your unfinished list, and shouldn't.

## Work is not the whole story

I love my work. I take it seriously. The job, the sermons, the follow-ups, the meetings that keep things moving. None of that is small to me. Ordinary work has dignity. Showing up for it matters.

But work is part of life, not the whole of it.

It's easy to forget that on a normal Monday. The calendar fills. The messages stack up. Everything feels urgent, even the things that are only loud. You can spend a whole morning treating productivity like identity and still miss the people sitting across the table from you.

I don't want that version of faithfulness.

Most of my week already has a home. Work. Studying for Sunday. Dates with my wife. Time with our kids. I love all of it. The danger isn't that any one of those is wrong. The danger is letting the loudest one crowd out the rest until the week looks full and life feels thin.

## What counted yesterday

Yesterday didn't look like output. It looked like presence. A birthday. A family in one room. A toast from a brother. Nothing that will show up in a status update, and nothing I'd trade for a cleaner inbox this morning.

That isn't a soft excuse to avoid responsibility. The emails will still be there. The tasks will still need owners. I'll still study, still lead, still do the quiet work that doesn't photograph well.

It's a reminder that clearing a list isn't the same as living a life. Titles don't hold me. Outcomes don't define me. Being useful is good. Being present with the people God gave me is better than being impressive to a screen.

My standing with God isn't improved by finishing everything on Monday, and it isn't reduced by leaving some of it for Tuesday. Faith in Christ is enough ground. The people I love aren't a distraction from my calling. They're part of it.

So yes, I'll open the laptop. I'll answer what needs answering. I'll keep the week honest.

But I'm starting slower than usual. The inbox will still be there.
