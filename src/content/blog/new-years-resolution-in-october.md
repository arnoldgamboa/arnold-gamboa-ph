Most people make resolutions in January. I'm making mine in October.

It's not because the calendar says so. It's because life doesn't wait for a clean start. Things get disrupted, seasons shift, and you notice something needs to change. I'd rather make those adjustments on the fly than wait three months for a date that feels more official.

So here's my list.

**Make more room for "me" time.** Most of my time already has a home. I work. I study for Sunday's preaching. I go on dates with my wife. I spend time with our kids. None of that is a bad thing, and I love every part of it.

But I've noticed I rarely have space in my mind that isn't already spoken for. I need room to look back, to reflect, to be creative, to think without an agenda. That isn't selfish. It's part of staying healthy enough to keep giving to the people I love.

**Spend more time with like-minded pastors.** In the past I've had great opportunities to be around other pastors who think and care about the same things I do. Over time, because of my schedule, I let those go.

I felt that loss most when problems came, in life and in church. In those seasons I wished I had like-minded church leaders around me who could encourage me and build me up. Pastors need pastoring too. I don't want to wait for the next hard season to rebuild those relationships.

**Spend more time with old friends.** Friends are a gift, and they carry a lot of us. It's easy to forget they're there when life gets busy. They don't disappear, but we stop reaching out.

I'm at a stage of life where I want to reconnect. I want to catch up, hear what's changed, and make new memories with people who knew me long before any of my current roles.

## Making room

Most of what fills my week is good, and I'm grateful for it. But good things can quietly crowd out other good things. This is me starting to make room, one small adjustment at a time.
