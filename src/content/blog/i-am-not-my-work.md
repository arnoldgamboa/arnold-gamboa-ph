I carry several titles without much effort: engineering manager, software builder, pastor. They are all true. They also make terrible foundations for a life.

![The Gospel of John]("/images/blog/i-am-not-my-work-the-gospel-of-john-2.webp")

A title can describe the work in front of me. It cannot tell me who I am. Neither can a shipped product, a promotion, a ministry platform, or a week when I somehow cleared the whole list. Those things move too easily. If I build my identity on them, I have to keep proving that I deserve to exist.

I know that pressure. A good outcome feels like permission to breathe. A stalled project feels personal. Someone else moves faster, gets noticed, or carries a larger platform, and the quiet comparison begins: Am I behind? Have I done enough? Does my work still matter?

Winning does not settle those questions for long. It only raises the next target.

## The freedom to say "I am not"

In John 1, priests and Levites ask John the Baptist, "Who are you?" He has a crowd and a message people are hearing. He could turn the moment into a platform. Instead, he answers with a series of denials.

"I am not the Christ."

"I am not Elijah."

"No."

They offer him identities, and he gives them back. There is no panic in his answer. John does not need to make himself larger because he knows his place in the story. He points away from himself: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world."

That order matters to me. John can speak plainly about who he is not because he knows who Jesus is. His security is not hiding inside his audience, his usefulness, or the outcome of his ministry.

Mine is not hiding in my work either.

![John 1:1–18]("/images/blog/i-am-not-my-work-john-1-1-18.webp")

## A better place to put that weight

I still care about doing good work. Grace is not an excuse for laziness, and calling does not make responsibility disappear. But work becomes cruel when I ask it to carry the weight of my identity.

A project may fail without making me a failure. A ministry season may stay small without becoming insignificant. I can lead a team without needing the role to prove my worth. I can pause a good idea without treating unfinished work as a verdict on my life.

This is especially hard when every part of life comes with a scorecard. Work has delivery dates. Products have users. Churches have attendance. The internet has numbers for nearly everything. Numbers can inform a decision, but they make poor names.

So I am keeping the old practice from this post. I write: I am not my title. I am not my productivity. I am not my ministry platform. I am not responsible for outcomes only God controls. I am not ahead or behind as a person.

Then I end where John points: I have seen the Lamb.

That is enough ground to stand on. The work still matters. It just does not get to name me.
