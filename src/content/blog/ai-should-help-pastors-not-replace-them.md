---I’m subscribed to several AI-for-churches mailing lists because I want to understand what pastors are being told right now.

This weekend, I got an email that stopped me.

The message was simple: ministry was busy, Sunday is coming, you still don’t have a sermon, and AI can write it for you. Full manuscript. Edit it to sound like you. Problem solved.

I understand why that message works.

Pastors are tired. Most people only see the sermon on Sunday. They don’t see the hospital visit, the counseling appointment, the late-night text, the staff issue, the funeral prep, the volunteer problem, the family responsibilities, and the blank page waiting at the end of all of it.

So when someone says, “It’s not your fault,” that lands.

And when someone says, “Let AI write it,” that sounds like relief.

But that is exactly where I think we need to be careful.

I believe pastors should use better tools. We already use technology for giving, slides, livestreaming, calendars, email, planning, and volunteer schedules. It would be strange to say sermon preparation and church communication are the only areas where technology cannot help.

But AI should not write the sermon for you.

I don’t say that because I’m anti-AI. I’m building YouPastor, so obviously I believe AI can help the church.

I say it because preaching is not content production.

A sermon is not just a religious speech that needs to be generated before Sunday. It comes from prayer, study, conviction, shepherding, and the burden God gives a pastor for a specific people in a specific moment.

AI can summarize a passage. It can surface background information. It can organize your notes. It can ask useful questions. It can help turn scattered thoughts into a clearer outline.

Those are good uses.

But it cannot carry the pastoral burden for your people.

It does not know the couple in the third row. It does not know the conversation you had after church last week. It does not know what God is confronting in your own heart through the text before you preach it to anyone else.

That’s why the promise of a fully AI-written sermon bothers me.

Not because the tool is useless. Because the shortcut is too tempting.

A sermon can be doctrinally acceptable and still not be yours. It can sound polished and still skip the slow work that forms the preacher.

Sermon preparation does not only produce sermons. It produces pastors.

That is why I’m building YouPastor.

Not as a robot preacher. Not as a generic sermon factory. Not as a way to outsource your calling.

I want to build a workspace where sermon ideas, passage research, outlines, illustrations, church communication, and weekly ministry tasks can stay connected.

A tool that helps pastors think, organize, and prepare without pretending to replace prayer, study, conviction, or care for people.

You stay the pastor.

It stays the tool.
